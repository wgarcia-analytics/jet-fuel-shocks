# Jet Fuel Shocks

**Question:** When planes stop flying, how far does jet fuel against diesel, and how long until it comes back?

**Why it matters:** Airlines often hedge jet fuel with heating oil futures. Anything that hits jet fuel but not diesel is a risk those hedges miss.

**Data:** Daily Gulf Coast jet fuel and diesel spot prices from the U.S. Energy Information Administration, via FRED.

**Events:** 9/11 (2001), COVID (2020), the FAA system outage (January 2023), the CrowdStrike outage (July 2024), and the FAA ground stop (November 2026)

**Finding:** 2026 Swing

Jet fuel and diesel split by 91 cents in four months. On May 18, 2026, jet fuel traded 27.5 cents above diesel. By September 17 it was 63.8 cents below, the lowest since the diesel data begins in 2006. That’s a 91.3-cent swing in about four months, driven mostly by diesel, which rose $1.24 a gallon while jet fuel rose 32 cents. A jet fuel hedge built on diesel futures couldn’t have covered it: on one 42,000-gallon contract, the swing is worth about $38,000.

**Finding:** Flight Disruption

Only a disruption that lasted months moved the market. In the 20 trading days after COVID hit, jet fuel fell 12.1 cents against diesel, 4.3 times a normal 20-day move, and in the direction falling demand should push it. The FAA outage (January 2023) and the CrowdStrike outage (July 2024) never moved the gap more than 1.4 times normal. Disruptions that last hours or days don’t show up in jet fuel prices.
